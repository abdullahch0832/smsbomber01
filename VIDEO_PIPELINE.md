### 0.3 What makes their videos win (patterns)

1. **Five content buckets** cover almost every outlier:
   - **A. "Banned / too strong / broke the rules"**: tactics, trades or games the league stopped. This bucket has the highest views on the channel.
   - **B. Underdog vs physical limits**: short Asian players who made the NBA. Regional pride is the hook.
   - **C. Bust / downfall mystery**: a #1 pick, a combine-record holder or a young champion who disappeared. *"Why?"*
   - **D. Hot-news explainer**: playoffs, trades, free agency. Published within 24–72h, and these get the steady 1.5–2x outliers.
   - **E. Legend peak breakdown**: "How dominant was X at his peak really?" (Duncan, Rodman, Hamilton).
2. **Local-audience angle wins.** Their single best recent outlier (3x) is about a *Taiwanese* player. Whatever your audience is, the player or story connected to *them* overperforms.
3. **Evergreen beats news on total views** (1M+ videos are all history or legacy). News keeps the channel alive day to day, while evergreen topics produce the big outliers.
4. **Consistency**: same length, same title formula, same thumbnail frame, daily uploads. The algorithm learned exactly who to show it to.

### 0.4 Title formula (exact structure)

Every title follows the same 5-part, very long (~70–90 Chinese characters) template:

```
[SHOCK FACT / RESULT]！ + [superlative descriptor] + [PLAYER NAME] + [究竟/到底/為何 … 有多X？] +
[proof point 1]、[proof point 2]， + [Famous person]：[short quote] ｜[Channel brand]
```

Example (translated):
> **"Officially halted for being too strong!** How absurd were the most creative genius tactics in NBA history?
> You can understand it but can't defend it or copy it, *as long as you have a Stephen Curry or Michael Jordan* | 澤北SG"

Ingredients that show up again and again:
- Opener with "！": a shocking fact, number, or result (`172cm made the NBA!`, `Banned by the league!`)
- Superlative: `史上最…` (most … in history), `唯一` (the only), `最恐怖` (most terrifying)
- Curiosity question: `到底有多離譜？` (how ridiculous was it really?), `為何…？` (why…?)
- **Specific numbers**: 34 rebounds, 51 fouls, 37 games, 172cm, 2 years
- **Quote tag at the end**: `Kobe：這輩子最不想防他` ("Kobe: the guy I least wanted to guard"). A famous name adds authority and gossip.

**Measured across 60 titles (30 most popular + 30 newest):**

| Feature | How often |
|---|---|
| Ends with `｜澤北SG` (brand) | 60/60 |
| Player/team named in the middle, English letters | ~58/60 |
| First clause ends with `！` (or `？！`) | ~60/60 |
| Central question word: `是否` (whether) / `究竟` / `到底` (really) / `為何` (why) | ~60/60 |
| Two proof points joined by `、` | ~55/60 |
| Ends with a quote `Name：…` | ~20/60 (more common in old popular videos) |
| Ends with a speculative `…？！` | ~20/60 (most new videos) |
| Contains a hard number (cm, %, years, $, games) | ~45/60 |
| Length | ~65–90 Chinese characters (very long on purpose) |

Recurring opening "shock" phrases: `竟然…` (shockingly…), `直接…` (straight up…), `再次…` (again…), `史上最…` (most … ever), `明明…` (even though…), `大概率…` (most likely…).
Recurring middle phrases: `…到底有多離譜？` (how absurd really?), `…是否真有機會/希望…？` (does X really have a chance…?), `…究竟是不是…最…？` (is X really the most…?).

In English the same formula has to be shorter (YouTube shows ~60–70 chars), so you split it into **Title** + **Thumbnail text** (see §3).

So a 10-minute video uses **~100 shots: about 55% video clips and 45% still photos.** That's roughly one new shot every 6 seconds.
For a 25-minute video in the same style you'd need **~250 shots (~140 clips + ~110 photos)**.

#### 0.6.2 The rhythm: clip/photo alternation

The edit alternates almost every shot: **clip → photo → clip → photo**.
- **Video clip** = the action the narration describes (game highlight).
- **Still photo** = the "talking" moments (background, quotes, transitions between ideas), with a slow zoom.
- Photos are held longer (6–9s) than clips (3–6s).
- In the skill-breakdown section, clips are shown in **slow motion** so a short clip covers a longer sentence.

#### 0.6.4 Footage sources seen in the video

- League broadcasts: CBA (Tencent/CCTV-5 style), FIBA Asia Cup and qualifiers, NCAA, NBA.
- News agency photos (action shots, portraits).
- Player's own Instagram/Weibo (childhood, training, photos with other players).
- A commercial/documentary-style clip (player in the gym, tying shoes) used for the outro.

#### 0.6.5 What this means for us

- Their editing is **simple and cheap to repeat**: subtitles + alternating clips/photos + zooms + hard cuts + 3 music moods. The consistency matters more than effects.
- Their clips run up to 13s. **Our rule is max 4s per clip** (see §6) to lower copyright risk, so we need more photos, slow-mo and graphics to fill the time.
- They barely use SFX, stat cards or graphics. That's where we can look **more polished** than them without much extra work.

## 3. Step 2: Title engineering

### 3.1 Formulas (from Sawakita, adapted to English length limits)

1. **Banned/too strong:** `The NBA Had to BAN This [Player/Tactic/Trade]`
2. **Physical-limit underdog:** `He's Only [5'8"], and He Still Made the NBA`
3. **Bust mystery:** `Champion at 23, Out of the NBA at 26: What Happened to [Player]?`
4. **Peak breakdown:** `How Unstoppable Was Prime [Player] Actually?`
5. **Most-X-ever:** `The Most [Violent/Absurd/Controversial] [Game/Trade] in NBA History`
6. **Number shock:** `[34] Rebounds in One Game: Why [Rodman] Played Basketball Like No One Else`
7. **Quote hook:** `"[Short quote]": The [Player] Story Nobody Talks About`

### 3.2 Rules

- 50–70 characters. Put the **name + hook in the first 40 chars**.
- At least one **specific number**.
- One curiosity gap. Never answer the question in the title.
- Write **10 variants** and pick by: clarity > curiosity > emotion.
- Thumbnail text ≠ title. Thumbnail text = **2–4 words** adding a second layer (nickname, number, or "?").

## 4. Step 3: Research pack (do this before writing)

Collect in one doc:
1. **Timeline**: 8–15 key dates/events.
2. **Stats**: 10+ hard numbers (career, peak season, record, contract).
3. **Quotes**: 3–6 real quotes from teammates, rivals, coaches (with source link).
4. **Turning points**: 3–5 moments where the story changes direction.
5. **Comparisons**: who they're compared to and why.
6. **Counter-view**: the strongest argument *against* your thesis.
7. **Sources list**: links for every number and quote.

> ⚠️ Fact-check rule: every stat and quote must have a source link. AI tools invent quotes, so never publish a quote you can't trace.

## 5. Step 4: 20–30 min script (the core)

### 5.1 Structure (≈ 3,600 words for ~24 min @ 150 wpm)

Sawakita does ~11 min with one deep breakdown. For 20–30 min, **repeat the "chapter" unit 4–6 times**, each with its own mini-hook and payoff, so retention doesn't collapse in the middle.

| # | Section | Time | Words | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cold open** | 0:00–0:30 | 70 | The most shocking moment/number of the whole story, shown first. End on a question. |
| 2 | **Catchphrase + promise** | 0:30–1:15 | 110 | Fixed brand line, then the news peg ("why now"), then 3 explicit questions this video will answer. |
| 3 | **Chapter 1: Origin** | 1:15–5:00 | 560 | Childhood/early career, the first sign of something special or wrong. |
| 4 | **Chapter 2: Rise** | 5:00–9:00 | 600 | Breakthrough, key stats, first big moment. |
| 5 | **Chapter 3: Turning point** | 9:00–13:00 | 600 | Conflict, injury, trade, controversy. Answer question #1 here. |
| 6 | **Chapter 4: Deep breakdown** | 13:00–18:00 | 750 | Skill/tactic/decision analysis with clips, comparisons, numbers. The highest-value section. |
| 7 | **Chapter 5: Consequences** | 18:00–21:30 | 520 | What happened after: legacy, league reaction, quotes. Answer question #2. |
| 8 | **Twist / counter-view** | 21:30–23:00 | 220 | "But here's what most people miss…" Answer question #3. |
| 9 | **Verdict + CTA** | 23:00–24:00 | 170 | Clear opinion, a question for the comments, then a next-video tease (not just "subscribe"). |

### 5.2 Retention devices (use all of them)

- **Open loop every 60–90 seconds**: "…but that wasn't even the worst part."
- **Chapter hook** at the start of each chapter: a rhetorical question (Sawakita's main transition tool).
- **Number every ~30 seconds**: each one becomes an on-screen stat card.
- **Re-hook at ~40% and ~70%** of runtime (where long videos drop): tease the twist.
- **Short sentences.** 8–15 words. Spoken rhythm, not essay rhythm.
- **No filler intros.** The first sentence must be the most interesting fact.
- **B-roll cues inline** in `[brackets]` so the editor knows exactly what to show.

```
{paste research pack}

Write the full narration script using this structure:
1 Cold open (70w) → 2 Catchphrase + news peg + 3 promised questions (110w) →

Rules:
- Start every chapter with a rhetorical question.
- Plant an open loop every 60–90 seconds; re-hook at 40% and 70% runtime.
- Include a specific number roughly every 30 seconds.
- Sentences of 8–15 words, written to be spoken aloud.
- After each sentence or two, add an editor cue in brackets:
  [CLIP: …], [PHOTO: …], [STAT CARD: …], [ARROW on …], [FREEZE], [MAP/GRAPHIC: …], [MUSIC: tense/uplifting/drop].

```

### 5.4 Script QA checklist

- [ ] First sentence = most shocking fact
- [ ] 3 questions promised in the first 75 seconds, all answered later
- [ ] Every chapter starts with a question
- [ ] Word count within ±10% of target
- [ ] Every stat/quote has a source; no `[VERIFY]` left
- [ ] B-roll cue at least every 2 sentences
- [ ] Read 2 random minutes aloud. If you get bored, rewrite them.

## 6. Step 5: Editing brief (Sawakita style)

Give this to your editor (or use it as a CapCut/Premiere template):

**Shot budget (copied from Sawakita's ratio, adjusted for our 4s clip rule):**

| Video length | Total shots | Video clips (≤4s each) | Still photos (5–7s, zoom) | Graphics / stat cards |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 min | ~130 | ~70 | ~45 | ~15 |
| 20 min | ~260 | ~140 | ~90 | ~30 |
| 25 min | ~320 | ~175 | ~110 | ~35 |
| 30 min | ~390 | ~210 | ~135 | ~45 |

**Hard rule: every video clip is 4 seconds or less.** Sawakita holds clips up to 13s. We don't. To cover long sentences without long clips:
- chain 2 different clips of 2–4s each, from different games/angles;
- slow a 2–3s clip to ~70% speed for the breakdown sections (still counts toward the 4s limit on screen);
- cut to a photo (Ken Burns) or a stat card in between;
- never use the same clip twice in one video.

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Format | 16:9, 1080p+ (4K if possible), faceless |
| Cut rate | New visual every **3–6 s**; clips max 4s, photos max 7s |
| Clip/photo rhythm | Alternate **clip → photo/graphic → clip** like Sawakita (~55% clips / ~35% photos / ~10% graphics) |
| Footage | Game highlights, press-conference clips, news photos, player social photos, stat graphics |
| Photos | Slow Ken Burns zoom (100%→110% over the shot) |
| Highlights | Arrow/circle on the subject player; **freeze frame + zoom** on key play |
| Stat cards | Every spoken number appears as bold text (e.g. `34 REB`), pop-in animation |
| Subtitles | Burned-in, bottom-center, bold sans, white with black stroke; highlight key words in brand color |
| Brand frame | A **constant colored border** (your color, not yellow) + logo watermark top corner |
| Music | Lo-fi/hip-hop bed at −20 to −25 dB; swell on highlights; switch to melodic/sad on struggle chapters; **beat drop** on reveals |
| SFX | Whoosh on transitions, impact hit on stat cards, record-scratch on twists (sparingly) |
| Transitions | Hard cuts mostly; quick zoom-cut between chapters + chapter title card |
| Chapter cards | 1.5 s full-screen chapter title at each section (helps retention + matches YouTube chapters) |
| Intro/outro | 3–5 s branded sting; end screen 20 s with 1 video + subscribe |

**Footage & copyright:** NBA/league broadcast footage is frequently Content-ID claimed. To lower risk:
use **short clips (max 4s)**, **mute the original game audio** (Sawakita also keeps it muted or faint), always **with commentary over them**, mix in photos/graphics/maps/stat
animations, avoid using broadcast audio, and keep the story/analysis as the main value. This
reduces the risk but does not remove it, so check claims in YouTube Studio before publishing.

## 7. Step 6: Thumbnail

### 7.1 Brief

- 1 hero player, **emotional or explosive moment**, cut out, right 60% of frame.
- Left side: **2–4 words**, max 2 lines, huge bold font, high-contrast stroke. A nickname, a number, or a short question.
- 1 accent element max (arrow, red circle, "?" or small inset of a rival).
- High saturation, readable at phone size. Test by viewing at 160px wide.
- Keep a **consistent brand element** (border color or corner tag) across all thumbnails.

## 9. Step 8: Publish & feedback loop

1. Publish at a fixed time daily (your audience's evening).
2. After 48h and 7 days, check in NexLev (connect your channel):
   - `get_my_top_videos`, `get_my_video_analytics` → views, CTR, AVD
   - `get_my_audience_retention` → **where people drop**. Fix that section type in the next script.
   - `get_my_traffic_sources` → browse vs search vs suggested
3. Log every video in a sheet: `title | bucket (A–E) | length | CTR | AVD% | views 7d | outlier vs your avg`.
4. Every 10 videos: double down on the bucket with the highest outlier score and drop the weakest.

**Benchmarks to aim for:** CTR ≥ 6%, AVD ≥ 35–40% on 20–30 min videos, first 30s retention ≥ 70%.
